Arabica vs. Robusta Beans

Understanding the two primary coffee species used across global specialty coffeehouses.

Coffea Arabica

Grown at higher elevations (900m–2,000m), Arabica beans mature slowly, developing complex sugars, bright fruit acidity, floral aromatics, and smooth chocolate notes. Arabica forms the backbone of our pour-over filter coffees and signature Lattes.

Coffea Canephora (Robusta)

Cultivated at lower altitudes with higher caffeine content, Robusta delivers bold earthiness, deep body, and thick, persistent crema. A carefully balanced touch of washed Robusta adds punch and crema stability to traditional espresso blends.

Brewing Methods Explained

Four distinct extraction techniques practiced at My Cafe.

9-Bar Espresso Extraction

Finely ground coffee is tamped evenly into a portafilter while pressurized 93°C water extracts soluble oils and sugars in 25–30 seconds. The result is a concentrated 30ml shot topped with emulsified golden crema.

Steamed Microfoam & Milk Texturing

Cold milk is aerated gently with dry steam to introduce microscopic air bubbles, then swirled into a glossy, paint-like texture at 60–65°C—unlocking natural lactose sweetness for Cappuccinos and Lattes.

Manual Pour-Over (V60)

Hot water is poured in slow concentric circles over medium-fine grounds in a ribbed paper-lined cone. Gravity draws a clean, nuanced cup highlighting delicate origin aromatics.

18-Hour Cold Immersion

Coarse coffee grounds steep in cold filtered water for 18 hours without heat. Cold extraction leaves behind bitter acids, creating a naturally sweet, smooth concentrate served over ice.
